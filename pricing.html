How licensing works.

What counts as a project

One project is one codebase you ship as a single product — not a fixed line count, and not tied to a specific repository host. If you maintain several genuinely separate products, each needs its own licence.

Duration and renewal

Licences run for a fixed term, currently annual. Renewing continues protection for future builds; it does not retroactively affect a jar you've already shipped under a previous term.

Device activations

A device activation is a machine authorised to run RJNI against your project — a developer workstation or a CI runner. Indie includes one; Team includes five. You can deactivate a device and activate another in its place.

Platform coverage

Every plan includes every platform RJNI supports — see Compatibility for exactly what that means today, including which targets are verified versus planned.

Support

Indie and Team both get email support, with Team prioritised. Enterprise terms — a direct channel and a response-time SLA — are agreed individually, since they depend on what you actually need.

Business and enterprise

Multiple projects under one agreement, custom activation limits, or a support SLA: email hello@rjni.dev directly rather than using the standard checkout.

Evaluation before you buy

Trial mode protects a deterministic, reproducible subset of your own project's eligible classes, so you can inspect real output — renamed classes, encrypted strings, nativized methods — before licensing the full build. See the trial mode documentation.
